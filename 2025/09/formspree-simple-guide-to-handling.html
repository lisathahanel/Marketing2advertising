Our Note: Before we start, let's understand the problem Formspree is designed to solve. Imagine that you have created a website with a Contact Us, Free Ad, Registration, or Feedback form. A visitor fills in the form and clicks Submit. But where does that information go?

Normally, a website needs some server-side programming to receive, process, and store the information. This can be complicated if your website is made only with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.

This is where Formspree comes in.

What Is Formspree?

Formspree is a third-party form service that allows a website to receive form submissions without you having to build and maintain your own server.

In simple terms, think of Formspree as a messenger between your website form and you.

A visitor enters their information on your website. When they click the submit button, Formspree receives the information, processes the submission, and can send the details to your email inbox or make them available through its dashboard.

👨‍🏫 Teacher's Example

Imagine your website has a contact form:

Visitor → Website Form → Formspree → Your Email/Dashboard

You don't need to build the server that sits in the middle. Formspree provides that service for you.

Why Would You Need Formspree?

Let's say you have a simple HTML website. You can easily create the visual part of a form, but HTML by itself does not provide a complete system for processing and managing submitted information.

Instead of creating your own backend system, Formspree gives your form an endpoint where the submitted information can be sent.

This makes it useful for websites built with technologies such as:

  • HTML and CSS
  • JavaScript
  • React
  • Webflow
  • Jamstack websites
  • Other static websites

How Does Formspree Work?

Let's break the process down into a simple story.

Step 1: A Visitor Opens Your Form

The visitor sees a form on your website. For example, it could ask for:

  • Name
  • Email address
  • Phone number
  • Message

Step 2: The Visitor Enters Information

The visitor fills out the form and clicks the submit button.

Step 3: The Form Sends the Information

Instead of sending the information to your own server, the form sends it to your unique Formspree endpoint.

Step 4: Formspree Processes the Submission

Formspree receives the submitted information and processes it according to your account and form settings.

Step 5: You Receive the Submission

Depending on your settings, you can receive notifications and manage submissions through your Formspree dashboard.

💡 Remember This

Your website creates the form. Formspree handles the form submission.

What Does "No Backend Required" Mean?

This phrase can sound complicated, so let's make it easy.

A backend is the part of a website that works behind the scenes. It can receive information, process it, save it, and perform other operations.

If you build a form without a service such as Formspree, you may need your own backend to receive and process the submitted information.

Formspree provides this form-processing infrastructure for you.

So when we say "No Backend Required", we mean that you don't have to create your own server-side form-processing system just to receive basic form submissions.

Important Formspree Features

1. No Backend Required

This is one of the main reasons people use Formspree. You can create a normal HTML form and send its information to a Formspree endpoint using an HTTP POST request.

This is especially useful for simple websites where you don't want to build a complete server-side application.

2. Spam Protection

Website forms can attract spam. Automated programs may repeatedly submit unwanted messages through your forms.

Formspree provides anti-spam features designed to help reduce unwanted submissions. Depending on the plan and configuration, these can include tools such as reCAPTCHA, honeypot techniques, and Formshield.

👨‍🏫 Simple Example

Think of spam protection like a security guard at a building entrance. The security guard does not guarantee that every unwanted visitor will be stopped, but it can help identify and block suspicious activity.

3. Integrations and Webhooks

Form submissions do not always have to remain inside one dashboard. Formspree can connect submissions with other services and workflows.

Depending on your Formspree configuration and plan, integrations can include services such as Slack, Airtable, Google Sheets, and Mailchimp.

This can be useful when several people need to work with the information submitted through your website.

4. Submission Management

Instead of searching through your email inbox for every form message, Formspree provides a dashboard where submissions can be managed.

Depending on the plan, features may include:

  • Searching submissions
  • Filtering submissions
  • Reviewing form data
  • Exporting information as CSV
  • Viewing available form analytics

How to Set Up Formspree

Now that we understand what Formspree does, let's walk through the setup process.

Step 1: Create a Formspree Account

First, create an account with Formspree and create a new form. Formspree will provide you with a unique endpoint for that form.

Your endpoint will look similar to: 

https://formspree.io/f/your-form-id

The exact URL is different for every form, so use the endpoint provided by your own Formspree account.

Step 2: Add the Endpoint to Your HTML Form

Your HTML form needs an action attribute containing your Formspree endpoint. You should also use the POST method. 

<form action="https://formspree.io/f/your-form-id" method="POST">

  <input type="text" name="name" placeholder="Your Name">

  <input type="email" name="email" placeholder="Your Email">

  <textarea name="message" placeholder="Your Message"></textarea>

  <button type="submit">Send Message</button>

</form>

Step 3: Understand the "action" Attribute

The action attribute tells the browser where the form information should be sent.

In this example: 

action="https://formspree.io/f/your-form-id"

the browser sends the form submission to Formspree.

📚 Think of "action" Like an Address

If you want to send a letter, you need an address. In an HTML form, the action attribute provides the destination where the form submission should be sent.

Step 4: Use Name Attributes

Every important form field should have a name attribute. This helps identify what the submitted value represents.

For example: 

<input type="text" name="name">

<input type="email" name="email">

<textarea name="message"></textarea>

Formspree can then identify the submitted information as a name, email address, message, or whatever field name you have specified.

Step 5: Test Your Form

After connecting your form, publish it and submit a test message.

Check your Formspree dashboard and your configured email notifications to make sure the submission is being received correctly.

⚠️ Teacher's Tip

Always test a form before sharing it with visitors. A form may look perfect on the screen but still have a configuration problem behind the scenes.
