How Starlink Works


Starlink uses a network of low Earth orbit (LEO) satellites for internet connectivity. These satellites are closer to the earth than traditional geostationary satellites. This results in lower latency and faster internet speeds.


The satellites communicate with a user terminal, or dish, at the user's location. This dish, affectionately known as "Dishy McFlatface," is a phased array antenna. It's capable of tracking multiple satellites simultaneously.


Key steps in Starlink's operation include:


Data is sent from the internet to a Starlink ground station.


The ground station sends the data to a Starlink satellite.


The satellite beams the data down to the user's dish.


The dish sends the data to the user's device via a Wi-Fi router.


This process ensures a seamless internet experience, even in the most remote locations.

Installation and Equipment


Getting Starlink up and running is a fairly straightforward process. The company provides all the necessary equipment, including the user terminal, mounting tripod, and Wi-Fi router.



The user terminal, also known as "Dishy McFlatface," is a phased array antenna. It automatically aligns itself to connect with Starlink's satellites. This makes the installation process easier for users.


However, it's important to choose the right location for the dish. It needs a clear view of the sky to function optimally. Obstructions like trees or buildings can affect the signal quality.
