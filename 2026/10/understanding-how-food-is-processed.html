Today we’re going to follow a piece of food on its journey through the body. I want you to picture this as a clear path: from the moment food enters the mouth until the waste leaves the body. Along the way we will see exactly how nutrients are absorbed and what happens when the system slows down as we age. Let’s walk through it together, step by step.

1. Digestion – Breaking Food Down into Usable Pieces

Everything begins in the stomach. The stomach is a strong, muscular, elastic, pear-shaped bag that sits in the upper abdomen. After you chew your food and swallow it, the food travels down a tube called the oesophagus and drops into the stomach.

Once the food arrives, the stomach gets to work. Powerful acids and digestive juices mix with the food and break it into tiny, usable pieces. Think of the stomach as a food processor that turns solid meals into a thick liquid mixture. This mixture is then ready to move on to the next stage — the intestines.

While the stomach is busy, the liver is also playing an important role. The liver helps with digestion by producing chemicals that the body needs, and it cleans the blood by removing impurities. In short, the liver acts like both a factory and a filter for the digestive system.

2. The Intestines – Where Nutrients Are Absorbed and Waste Is Prepared

The intestines (also called the bowel or colon) form the main part of both digestion and waste elimination. They are divided into two clear sections: the small intestine and the large intestine.

The Small Intestine – The Nutrient Absorption Centre

Most of the real digestion and almost all nutrient absorption happens in the small intestine. The walls of the small intestine are lined with tiny finger-like projections that act like sponges. As the liquid food mixture passes through, vitamins, minerals, proteins, carbohydrates, and fats are absorbed through these walls and enter the bloodstream. From there they are carried all around the body to give us energy and keep every organ working.

So, if you remember only one thing about the small intestine, remember this: this is where the body takes the nutrients it needs from food.

The Large Intestine – Absorbing Water and Moving Waste

Whatever is left after the small intestine has finished its work moves into the large intestine. Here the body absorbs any remaining water and a few leftover nutrients. At the same time, the large intestine pushes the leftover waste material along until it is ready to leave the body. This is the final stage of the digestive process.

3. How Ageing Changes Digestion and What We Can Do About It

As people grow older, several changes take place that make digestion slower and more difficult.

  • The stomach produces less acid and less saliva. Less acid means food is not broken down as easily.
  • Bowel action slows down, which often leads to indigestion and constipation.
  • Many older people also lose teeth, which makes chewing harder and means food reaches the stomach in larger pieces.

These changes are normal, but they can cause discomfort and health problems if we do not adapt the care we give.

Practical Care Tips for Clients with Digestive Difficulties

When you are looking after someone whose stomach, intestines, or liver are not working as well as they used to, keep these simple rules in mind:

  • Avoid foods that are hard to digest or difficult to chew. Soft, well-cooked meals are usually easier and safer.
  • If the person is constipated, increase their fluid intake. Extra water helps keep the bowel contents soft and moving.
  • Add fresh fruit, vegetables, whole grains, and fibre to their diet. Prunes are especially useful because they have a natural laxative effect.
  • Encourage gentle exercise. Even a short walk or simple movements can stimulate the bowel and improve digestion.

Fruit and vegetables are particularly helpful for treating constipation because they provide both fibre and natural moisture.

Let’s quickly recap the journey we followed today:

  • Food is chewed, swallowed, and broken down by acids in the stomach.
  • The small intestine absorbs most of the nutrients and sends them into the bloodstream.
  • The large intestine absorbs water and moves the remaining waste out of the body.
  • As we age, less acid, slower bowel action, and missing teeth make digestion harder — so we adapt the diet, increase fluids, and encourage movement.
