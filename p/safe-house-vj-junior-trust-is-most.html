In the chaos that follows a massive terrorist attack in downtown Los Angeles 💥, six government agents find themselves locked down inside a high-security safe house — a fortress meant to protect them from the outside world. But as the hours drag on and tensions rise, it becomes clear that the true threat might already be inside.

What starts as a fight for survival quickly turns into a psychological standoff. Each agent carries secrets, hidden motives, and questionable loyalties. Is the enemy truly outside the walls — or hiding behind a familiar face? 🤔

At its core, Safe House isn’t just a story about espionage or survival — it’s a sharp, claustrophobic look at trust, paranoia, and the fragility of human connection under pressure. The walls that are supposed to keep danger out instead trap fear and suspicion in. Every whispered conversation feels loaded, every gesture carries double meaning.

Stylistically, the concept evokes the tension of classics like The Thing, 10 Cloverfield Lane, and Reservoir Dogs — stories that confine their characters in tight spaces until the cracks start to show. The atmosphere is thick with unease: flickering lights, muffled explosions in the distance, and the ever-present question of who will break first.
