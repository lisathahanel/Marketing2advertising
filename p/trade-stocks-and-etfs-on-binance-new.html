Let’s talk about something exciting… the way people invest is changing, and it’s changing fast.

For a long time, stocks and crypto lived in two completely separate worlds. If you wanted to trade stocks, you needed a traditional brokerage account. If you wanted crypto, you went to an exchange. Two platforms, two systems, two different experiences.

But now, things are starting to merge—and that’s where Binance steps in with something new.

Imagine opening just one app and having access to both crypto and traditional markets. No switching platforms, no complicated setup. Just one place where you can explore more opportunities.

That’s the idea behind Binance Stock Trading.

So… Can You Really Buy Stocks Using Crypto?

Here’s where it gets interesting.

Yes, you can actually buy stocks using crypto—but there’s a small twist that makes everything work smoothly behind the scenes.

When you place an order, your crypto—whether it’s USDT, BNB, or another supported asset—is automatically converted into USDC. Think of USDC as the “bridge” currency that connects crypto with stock trading.

It’s kind of like traveling to another country. You might have your local currency, but before you buy anything, it gets exchanged into the currency used there. Same concept here.

Once that conversion happens, your order is processed, and just like that—you’ve stepped into the stock market.

And when you sell your stocks? The money comes back to you as USDC in your account.

What Can You Actually Trade?

Now you might be wondering… “Okay, but what stocks are available?”

Well, Binance—through a partnership with an external brokerage—offers access to over 7,000 stocks and ETFs.

That means you’re not limited to just a few options. You can explore a wide range of companies and funds, including some of the biggest brands in the world.

Think of it like walking into a massive global marketplace instead of a small local shop. The variety alone opens up more possibilities for your investment strategy.

When Can You Trade?

Here’s another feature that makes things flexible.

Stock trading on Binance can run up to 24 hours a day, 5 days a week. Now, not every stock supports full 24-hour trading—but many do.

And when placing an order, you can choose how you want to participate:

  • Regular Trading Hours – This is when the main market is open.
  • Extended Hours – Includes pre-market and after-market sessions.
  • 24-Hour Trading – Covers overnight and all sessions combined.

Think of it like a store that doesn’t just open during the day—it also has early access hours and late-night options. You get more flexibility depending on your schedule.

What About Fees?

Let’s address something everyone cares about—fees.

Binance does not charge commissions for stock trading. That means you’re not paying a fixed fee per trade like on some traditional platforms.

However, there are still costs to be aware of. These can include things like a platform fee or spread, which is essentially the difference between buying and selling prices.

And depending on the stock, there may also be other charges—like taxes, dividend-related fees, or regulatory costs.

So while it’s simple on the surface, it’s always a good idea to understand the details before you start trading.

How Does Stock Ownership Work?

This is an important part, so let’s break it down clearly.

When you buy stocks through Binance, you are the beneficial owner of those shares. That means the investment is yours—you can benefit from dividends and corporate actions.

However, the shares themselves are held in custody by a partnered brokerage.

If that sounds confusing, think of it like storing valuables in a secure vault. You still own them, but they’re kept safely by a trusted third party.

Understanding Orders and Trading Basics

When placing trades, you’ll typically come across two main types of orders:

  • Market Orders – These execute immediately at the current market price.
  • Limit Orders – These let you set the price you’re willing to buy or sell at.

There’s also support for fractional trading on selected stocks. That means you don’t always need to buy a full share—you can invest smaller amounts, starting from as little as $5.

This makes it more accessible, especially if you’re just getting started.

Trading Sessions Made Simple

The market isn’t just “open” or “closed.” It moves through different sessions throughout the day.

During the main market hours, everything is active. But outside those hours—like pre-market or overnight—some orders may be queued or processed differently.

It’s a bit like placing an order at a restaurant before it opens. Your order is recorded, but it only gets prepared once the kitchen is running.

Understanding these sessions helps you know when your trades will actually go through.

A Quick Word on Risk

Before we wrap up, let’s keep things real.

Investing—whether in crypto or stocks—comes with risk. Prices can go up, but they can also go down.

There’s no guarantee of profit, and it’s always possible to lose money.

That’s why it’s important to make informed decisions, start small if needed, and never invest more than you can afford to lose.

Binance is bringing together two worlds—crypto and traditional finance—into one platform, making it easier for users to explore more opportunities without jumping between apps.

With access to thousands of stocks, flexible trading hours, and the ability to start with small amounts, it opens the door for more people to participate in global markets.

In simple terms: it’s about convenience, access, and giving you more control over how and where you invest.

And as finance continues to evolve, tools like this are shaping what the future of investing looks like.
